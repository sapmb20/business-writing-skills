# Blogs and Social Media

A post is the most public thing most people write at work, and the hardest to take back.
The advice here comes down to three things: write conversationally, post on a schedule
someone will keep, and assume everyone (your employer, a client, a recruiter, a regulator)
will read it eventually.

Sources: standard business-communication practice (see ATTRIBUTION.md at the repo root),
plus the regulators' own guidance cited in §9. The platform notes, finance additions, and
"easy to confuse" list are this skill's own, labeled as such.

## 1. Blogs and forums

**A blog** (short for "web log") is a site where a person or a team publishes dated posts,
newest first: news, opinion, how-tos, and replies to other writers.

- Give it a **defined focus, audience, and purpose.**
- Set a **regular posting schedule** and keep it.
- Before starting, survey the blogging platforms and talk with IT and marketing about how a
  blog would serve the organization.

**A forum** is a wider conversation. Visitors don't just comment on posts; they start new
topics and threads. Forums often serve as a company's customer or tech-support desk, so
**monitoring and answering are essential.** A forum must be promoted, fed with content, and
used to draw content from users, or it quickly loses its value and dies.

## 2. What organizations use them for

**Five goals:**

1. attract and keep customers or clients;
2. build goodwill;
3. get useful feedback on products and services;
4. support customers;
5. build community among customers and employees.

**External vs. internal:**

- **External** blogs and forums are public. Executives, spokespeople, and staff use them to
  talk with customers, and customers use them to talk with each other; that direct line, and
  news that appears nowhere else first, is what builds loyalty.
- **Internal** ones sit on the intranet for employees only. They work as an interactive
  newsletter that builds community, or as a channel for breaking news on products, benefits,
  or new team members.

## 3. Writing style for posts

- **Informal and conversational:** contractions, first person, active voice (`line-editing`
  on voice and tone).
  - *Blog:* "We just moved the Q3 webinar to Thursday so more of you on the West Coast can
    make it. Tell us what times work best."
  - *Forum:* "Same issue here after the update. Clearing the cache fixed it for me. Has anyone
    found a fix that sticks?"
- **Concise** sentences and paragraphs.
- **Built for scanning:** bulleted lists, italics, boldface, and white space where the
  platform allows (`lists-and-headings`).
- **Headlines short, meaningful, and direct.** They catch attention and make the page easier
  to read.
- **Links** to other sites and resources participants will find useful.
- **As it grows,** organize with **categories** (links to discussion topics) and **tags**
  (keywords for searching posts).

## 4. Ethics: five rules for anything you post

The organization expects you to take full responsibility for what you post on a company
blog or forum. The same rules apply to social media.

1. **Nothing confidential, proprietary, or sensitive** to the employer.
2. **No attacks** on competitors and no abusive language toward other participants, even
   while making a strong point.
3. **Nothing profane, libelous, or harassing,** and nothing that violates someone's privacy
   (`line-editing` on biased language).
4. **Assume permanence.** Everything posted, especially on an external site, stays
   accessible to a wide public.
5. **Get permission** for copyrighted, patented, or trademarked material, and **credit the
   source** of every quotation (`research-and-sources`).

## 5. Social media

Sites and apps where people and organizations form online communities to create content,
interact, and share. They are reached by browser or phone, often include messaging and email
features, and many carry blogs and forums.

**For an individual:**

- Build a professional presence and network in your field.
- Keep **profiles consistent across sites, including the photo,** so employers and colleagues
  can find and recognize you.
- Keep the tone of the profile and posts professional. If your accounts have been personal,
  consider **separate work-focused accounts.**
- Network actively: many fields hold **scheduled hashtag chats** on current events and
  mentoring.

**For an organization:** writers run accounts to connect with clients, share product
information, and reach new customers. Social media builds goodwill, resolves problems, and
gets near-instant feedback.

**Choosing platforms.** Weigh what you want to accomplish against **where your target
customers or contacts actually are.**

- A manufacturer might pick a platform centered on users of similar products.
- A service business might pick one where users can ask for and get help right away.
- A small shop might use several: an image-heavy platform to show products, and a
  professional one for the owner's network.

**Two features matter most at work:**

- **Status updates** (posts): brief announcements or answers that others can respond to
  immediately.
- **Networks** (connections, follows): linking one person or organization to another, so their
  posts join a continuing shared conversation.

**Three cautions before you join:**

1. **You can't control or pause the conversation.** You must be willing to answer inquiries
   and comments, good and bad.
2. **It takes time.** Users expect quick, continuing responses and updates. In a crisis,
   someone must monitor the account **outside business hours.**
3. **The audience is wider than you intend,** even with audience-limiting settings.

## 6. Style and privacy

- Follow your organization's practices **and** the platform's own conventions.
- Mind the context, purpose, and audience of each message; make it clear, precise, and free of
  grammatical errors (`grammar-and-mechanics`).
- Posts spread at once, often widely, and onto unrelated sites. Weigh the benefit to your
  immediate audience **and** what the post would mean to people outside your network.
- "Informal" platforms still represent you professionally, because anything can be shared.
- Employers review applicants' profiles; organizations pay services to monitor what's said
  about them; many forbid employees to discuss the workplace even on personal accounts.

## 7. Judicious-use checklist

- [ ] **Purpose and fit.** Is this suitable to post at all? Keep topics that belong in a
      one-on-one conversation, and divisive topics, off public feeds.
- [ ] **Everyone can see it.** How would your employer or school read this update or photo?
- [ ] **Employer policy.** Follow it; working around it can cost you the job.
- [ ] **No complaints about your job, your employer, a client, or a teacher.** Assume the
      person you mention will read it.
- [ ] **Connection requests, considered.** Weigh company policy, the professional relationship,
      and any present or future conflict of interest before accepting.
- [ ] **At least one public, professional account,** especially while job hunting. Employers
      search candidates; finding nothing can look like hiding something.

Then apply the five ethics rules in §4.

## 8. Choosing a platform

| Platform | What it is mainly used for |
| --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn** | The professional network: a profile that works as a résumé, posts and articles, groups, company pages, and job listings. The main platform for finance and professional audiences |
| **Facebook** | Consumer brand pages and groups: posts, photos, video, events, customer interaction |
| **X** (formerly Twitter) | Short, fast public updates: breaking news, announcements, live conversation with customers. Posts are limited to 280 characters on free accounts |
| **Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts** | Short video and images, now central for consumer brands |
| **Substack, company-site blogs, LinkedIn newsletters** | Long-form posts and subscriber newsletters |

Platforms change their limits and features often. Check them on the platform before
relying on any of these.

## 9. Finance additions (this skill's own)

Verify the current rule before relying on any of these.

- **Reg FD and social media.** The SEC's 2013 report (after a CEO's personal Facebook post
  disclosed a material metric) said companies may disclose material information through
  social media **only if investors have been told in advance which channels the company
  uses.** Without that notice, put material news out by press release and 8-K first.
- **No material nonpublic information.** Never post, hint at, or "like" MNPI, including deal
  rumors from your own work. Analysts and bankers usually need **compliance pre-clearance**
  for anything about covered companies or the firm.
- **Broker-dealers (FINRA).** FINRA's social-media guidance treats **static content**
  (profiles, posted articles) like advertising, which typically needs principal approval
  before posting. **Interactive content** (live replies) is supervised like correspondence.
  Business communications must be **kept as records,** so use only approved,
  archive-captured platforms for business.
- **Advisers:** the SEC Marketing Rule covers testimonials and performance in posts, as in
  `promotional-writing`.
- **Endorsements (FTC).** Disclose any material connection clearly in the post itself
  ("#ad", "I'm paid by...", "I work here"), not only in a profile.
- **Reading company posts as an investor.** An executive's post is promotion, not disclosure,
  unless the company has named that channel. Check it against filings.

## 10. Easy to confuse (standard practice)

- **Blog vs. forum:** the blogger sets the topics and readers comment; in a forum, anyone can
  start a topic, and the host must moderate and seed it.
- **Status update vs. network:** a post vs. the connections that carry it.
- **External vs. internal blog:** a public site for customers vs. an intranet site for
  employees.
- **Categories vs. tags:** a few broad topic links vs. many specific search keywords.
- **Personal vs. professional account:** separate them, but assume both will be read by an
  employer.
- **Disclosure vs. promotion (finance):** a recognized-channel announcement vs. a post that
  happens to mention results.

## 11. Before you post

- [ ] Right channel (`selecting-medium`) and right platform for this audience
- [ ] One clear point; conversational, active, concise; a short direct headline
- [ ] Nothing confidential, abusive, libelous, or privacy-violating; would survive being read
      by your employer, a client, and a stranger
- [ ] Sources credited; images and material licensed
- [ ] Facts and numbers checked; for finance, no MNPI, compliance approval if required,
      connections disclosed
- [ ] Someone will monitor and answer replies, including after hours if it could go wrong
- [ ] Proofread (`grammar-and-mechanics`)
