# The business-writing family: what each sibling holds

Part of `business-writing`. The full catalog of the family: what each sibling skill holds and
the step where you load it. The always-on routing tree is in the skill body ("The
business-writing family"); read this file when choosing between two siblings that seem to
overlap, or when you need to know whether a sibling covers a topic before loading it.

| Skill | What it holds | Load it at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `selecting-medium` | The six factors for choosing a channel, talk-vs-write rules, what each medium is for (email, memo, letter, text and chat, call, voice mail, meetings, web conferences, intranet, website, social media) | Step 1.4, every time the medium isn't already fixed |
| `email-writing` | Subject lines with the call to action, one thread per topic, greetings, main point before context, the request, hyperlinks, CC etiquette, follow-ups, pre-send checks, client settings (default Reply, 30-second undo send), a thread-reply reference for replies inside a thread; correspondence types (inquiries, acknowledgments, cover messages, reference letters); templates for a request email, a follow-up, and a full-block letter | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the medium is email |
| `memo-writing` | The memo heading block, the body sequence (opening, context, call to action, discussion, closing, summary, attachments), ask-early vs. findings-first order, the report memo's four parts and report types (investigative, feasibility, progress, activity, incident), format and tone, pre-send checklist; a memo template | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the medium is a memo or the task is a report memo |
| `reports-and-proposals` | Formal reports (front, body, back matter), the report components (executive summary recipe, abstracts, introductions, conclusions, appendixes, titles), feasibility and trip reports, proposals (internal, RFP/IFB, unsolicited, sales, grant), business plans, annual reports; templates for an executive summary, formal report outline, internal proposal, RFP response, progress report, and incident report | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the document is a formal or multi-section report, a proposal, a business plan, or an investment write-up with an executive summary |
| `promotional-writing` | The six-point promotional checklist, sales letters (four moves, benefits over features, softening price), brochures (panel plan), press releases (five W's, dateline, # # #), newsletters and articles, mission statements; finance rules (Reg FD, safe harbor, FTC, FINRA); press-release and sales-letter templates | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the piece promotes a company, product, or service, or announces news |
| `writing-for-the-web` | Writing for scanners, seven web-content techniques (keywords up front), links and vetting outside sites, posting documents, FAQs, repurposing; SEO and accessibility notes; an FAQ page template | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the medium is a web or intranet page or FAQ, or content is being adapted to a new medium |
| `blogs-and-social-media` | Blogs vs. forums, conversational post style, the five posting-ethics rules, choosing platforms, the three cautions, the judicious-use checklist; finance rules (Reg FD channels, MNPI, FINRA) | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the medium is a blog, forum, or social post |
| `instructions-and-procedures` | Instructions (imperative, numbered, tested), process explanations, policies and procedures (the actor/action chart), forms (entry types, sequencing, question types), job descriptions; SOP and job-description templates | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the document tells someone how to do something, sets a policy, collects information on a form, or describes a job |
| `visuals` | Choosing the visual by its job (table, line, bar, pie, picture graph, Gantt, flowchart, org chart, drawing, photo, map, infographic), table anatomy, graph rules, integrating visuals with text, misleading scales, finance-chart conventions, global graphics | Step 4 (where visuals go) and whenever a document or deck needs a chart or table |
| `presentations` | Purpose statement (know, believe, do; what and why), audience analysis for listeners, the listener-shaped structure (opening, overview, body, transitions, closing), the five attention-getting openings, slide rules and slide design (one message, six objects or fewer, no sentences on screen, size and contrast steer the eye), rehearsal, delivery (eye contact, movement and the three t's, gestures, voice, projection, pace), anxiety, cross-cultural audiences; meetings (agendas, difficult participants, minutes, active listening) with an agenda-and-minutes template | Step 1.4 onward, whenever the medium is a presentation, pitch, or slide deck |
| `virtual-presentations` | Sub-skill of `presentations`: lighting, background, framing, the camera as eye contact, sound and tech backups, co-host, engagement every ≤10 minutes, recorded solo talks, pre-session checklist | Whenever the presentation is online or recorded |
| `circling-back` | Sub-skill of `presentations`: the follow-up email after a talk, with the action items (usually the last slide) as a short list of action, owner, and date; loads `email-writing`, `lists-and-headings`, `line-editing` | After a presentation or meeting that ended with next steps |
| `delivering-bad-news` | Choosing the indirect pattern (customers, employees) or the upward pattern (your boss); the five and four parts of each; common failures; refusal, adjustment, complaint, and collection letters; bad-news letter and complaint-response templates | Step 3, whenever the message delivers bad news |
| `research-and-sources` | Primary and secondary research, evaluating sources (authority, accuracy, bias, currency), note-taking, interviews and questionnaires, quoting and paraphrasing, plagiarism and common knowledge, copyright and fair use, a citation-basics reference (APA models, finance-source models, APA vs. MLA vs. Chicago) | Step 2, whenever the piece uses, quotes, or cites sources |
| `career-writing` | Skills by career, evidence over labels, proof points, STAR stories, finance-role priorities, offers; job-search documents (résumé sections and ATS formatting, cover letters, thank-yous, acceptances, resignations); a one-page finance résumé and cover-letter template | Steps 1 and 2 of any recruiting task |
| `lists-and-headings` | List types, punctuation, lead-ins, heading design and mistakes; page design (proximity, contrast, repetition, alignment, type, white space) | Step 4, and revision pass 5 |
| `grammar-and-mechanics` | Proofreading in stages, sentence faults, agreement, case, that/which, punctuation (13 marks), numbers, money, dates, abbreviations, finance-number conventions, commonly confused words; a usage reference for every mark and mechanic | Revision pass 7, and any grammar, punctuation, or usage question |
| `line-editing` | Wordiness, signs of weakness, key rules, active vs. passive voice, parallel structure; style and tone rules; a plain-language reference (federal guidelines and the SEC's plain-English handbook, with paragraph unity and transitions) | Revision pass 4 |
