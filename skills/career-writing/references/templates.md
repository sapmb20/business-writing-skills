# Finance résumé and cover-letter templates

Part of `career-writing`. A one-page résumé and a cover letter for finance and banking
roles. If the user has a template from a school, club, or firm, use that instead and apply
this skill's bullet rules to it.

> **Source and license.** Compiled for this repo (CC BY 4.0, see LICENSE-CONTENT). The
> layout was checked element by element against two university career-center sources:
> - the Harvard Mignone Center for Career Success bullet-point résumé template (2025);
> - the University of Pennsylvania Career Services undergraduate résumé checklist and
>   "Anatomy of a Cover Letter".
>
> Both are copyrighted, so they are cited and not copied. The finance-specific rules are
> marked as repo conventions in the tables below. Bullet guidance comes from
> `career-writing` ("Replace skill labels with evidence", STAR).

## 1. One-page résumé

Letter size, margins 0.5-1 inch, one serif or sans-serif face, name 16-20 pt, body 10-11 pt.
Put dates and locations on a right-aligned tab stop, not spaces. Single column, standard
headings, no tables or text boxes, so applicant tracking systems can parse it.

```
                              FIRST LAST
          City, ST · (555) 555-0100 · first.last@example.com · linkedin.com/in/firstlast

EDUCATION
University Name                                                            City, ST
B.S. in [Major], Concentration in [Finance]                           Expected May 20XX
• GPA: X.XX/4.00 [include when it helps]; [test scores, if strong and expected]
• Honors: [Dean's List (semesters), scholarships]
• Relevant coursework: [Corporate Finance, Financial Accounting, Investments, ...]

EXPERIENCE
Firm Name                                                                  City, ST
Title, Group                                                         Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY
• [Verb] [what you did] [how / with what], [result with a number], no closing period
• [Built a three-statement model for a $40M add-on acquisition, used in the final IC memo]
• [Two lines at most per bullet; three to five bullets for the most relevant role]

Firm Name                                                                  City, ST
Incoming [Title]                                                          Summer 20XX
                                                         (a role not yet started: no bullets)

LEADERSHIP
Organization                                                               City, ST
Role                                                                 Mon YYYY – Present
• [What you led or changed, and what came of it: members, money raised, events, outcomes]

SKILLS & INTERESTS
Technical: [Excel (financial modeling), Python, Bloomberg, Capital IQ, ...]
Certifications: [e.g. Bloomberg Market Concepts, FMVA]
Languages: [Fluent in X; conversational in Y]
Interests: [Two or three specific ones a reader could ask about]
```

Rules that matter most for finance readers:

- **Evidence, not labels:** every bullet shows a skill through a specific act and result.
- **Numbers:** deal size, dollars, percentages, counts, time saved, wherever true.
- **Reverse chronological** within each section; the most relevant role gets the most room.
- **Mechanics:** consistent date format (Jan 2025 – Sep 2025, spaced en dash); past tense
  for past roles, present for current; no personal pronouns; no closing periods on bullets
  (or periods on all, consistently).
- **Low GPA:** don't drop the line silently if the employer expects one; flag it to the user.

**How this was compiled**

| Element | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Section order: Education, Experience, Leadership & Activities, Skills & Interests | Harvard bullet-point template (identical order); Penn checklist ("Your first section is 'Education'") |
| Organization and city on one line, title and dates on the next, aligned to a tab stop | Harvard template (tab-aligned); Penn checklist (consistent alignment; two-letter state abbreviations) |
| GPA, relevant coursework, and honors under Education | Harvard template (GPA optional); Penn checklist (list GPA if above 3.00, with all three digits) |
| Reverse chronological order | Harvard template ("Beginning with your most recent position"); Penn checklist |
| Bullets start with an action verb, quantify, and use no personal pronouns; phrases, not sentences | Harvard template; Penn checklist ("strong, active verbs") |
| Consistent date format and dashes | Penn checklist (en dash vs. hyphen) |
| Margins and font size | Penn checklist: margins at least 0.65 in, font at least 10 pt. This template's 0.5-in minimum margin is **tighter than Penn's**; use 0.65 in or more if your school follows Penn. |
| Leadership above Experience when it's more relevant | Harvard template |
| Skills section: technical skills, languages, interests | Harvard template; Penn checklist |
| One page; "Incoming [Title]" with no bullets; Certifications line; deal-size numbers in bullets; no photo or objective | Repo conventions for US finance recruiting (not in either source) |
| Single column, no tables or text boxes | Repo convention, so that applicant tracking systems can parse the file |

## 2. Cover letter

Under one page; three or four short paragraphs.

```
[Your name]
[City, ST · phone · email]

[Month D, YYYY]

[Recipient's name, title]
[Firm]
[City, ST]

Dear [Dr./Ms./Mr./Mx. Last name]:       (no name findable? "Dear Hiring Manager:",
                                         never "To Whom It May Concern")

[Why you're writing and why this firm, specifically: the role, how you heard of it (a
named contact, if they agreed), and one concrete reason this firm and group, such as a deal,
a strategy, or a conversation.]

[Proof, part one: your strongest experience as a short STAR story that matches the
posting's top requirement, with a number.]

[Proof, part two: a second skill the posting asks for (technical, leadership, or teamwork),
with evidence, and what you'd bring to the team.]

[The ask: "I would welcome the chance to discuss how I could contribute to [group]." Offer
any further materials, and thank the reader. Don't propose a specific day or time.]

Sincerely,
[Name]
```

Before sending: the firm's name is right everywhere (a wrong name is the most common fatal
error); every claim matches the résumé; under 400 words.

**How this was compiled**

| Element | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Return address, date, recipient block, "Dear [Title] Last name:" | Penn, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter" |
| No name findable: "Dear Hiring Manager", never "To Whom It May Concern" | Penn, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter" |
| Paragraph 1: the role, how you heard of it, a named contact, and specific research on the firm | Penn, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter" |
| Paragraphs 2-3: what you'll contribute, with evidence matched to the posting; don't repeat the résumé | Penn, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter" |
| Close: ask for an interview without naming a day or time; offer materials; thank the reader | Penn, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter" |
| STAR proof with a number; under 400 words; firm name checked everywhere | Repo conventions (`career-writing`) |

**Source:** University of Pennsylvania Career Services, "Anatomy of a Cover Letter",
https://careerservices.upenn.edu/resources/anatomy-of-a-cover-letter/
