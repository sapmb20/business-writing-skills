# Bad-news templates

Part of `delivering-bad-news`. Two fill-in templates: an indirect bad-news letter (a
refusal) and a complaint response (an adjustment).

> **Source and license.** Compiled for this repo (CC BY 4.0, see LICENSE-CONTENT). The
> wording and examples are new; the structure follows *Communication at Work* by Julie
> Rivers (eCampusOntario, 2022), sections 8.2 and 8.3,
> https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/comm1062/, licensed
> [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Each element is credited in the
> "How this was compiled" table under its template. Where this repo departs from the
> source, the table says so and why.

## 1. Indirect bad-news letter or email (refusal)

Use for customers, applicants, or employees receiving a decision already made.

```
Subject: Your [request / application / proposal] for [topic]     (neutral; don't announce the no)

Dear [Name]:

[1. Buffer. Something true and relevant that doesn't hint at a yes:
 "Thank you for proposing a shared-services pilot for the Midwest region, and for the
 detailed cost model."]

[2. Reasons, from the reader's side, specific enough that the decision follows from them:
 "This year's budget review set two conditions for new pilots: payback within 12 months
 and no new headcount. Your model shows payback in 20 months and adds two analysts."]

[3. The news, once, clearly, in the middle of a paragraph, without apology:
 "For those reasons we can't fund the pilot in 2027."]

[4. Redirection: a real alternative, if one exists:
 "If the scope can shrink to the two largest sites, payback falls inside 12 months, and
 we'd review a revised model at the March cycle."]

[5. Positive closing, looking forward; no second apology, no invitation to argue:
 "The analysis behind this was among the clearest we saw this year."]

Sincerely,
[Name, title]
```

Check before sending: a reader who skims still knows the answer; the alternative is real;
nothing promises what the writer can't deliver; nothing admits fault without legal review.

**Use the direct order instead** (the news, a brief reason, a goodwill close) when the news
is minor, when the reader prefers it, when space is short, when an earlier indirect message
was ignored, or when the reader might miss buried news.

## 2. Complaint response (adjustment)

### Full adjustment (you grant the claim): good news first

```
Subject: Re: [their subject line]

Dear [Name]:

[The yes, first: "A replacement [item] shipped today by overnight delivery, at no charge;
tracking number [number]."]

[Details of compliance, and what you changed so it won't happen again: "A packing error at
our Memphis warehouse sent the 2026 model; we have added a model check before sealing."]

[What the customer needs to do, if anything: "Please keep or recycle the original; no
return is needed."]

[Close with confidence in the relationship, without recalling the problem.]

Sincerely,
[Name, title, direct phone]
```

### Partial adjustment (the claim is only partly valid): explanation first

```
Dear [Name]:

[Buffer: thanks for the detail in the message, and what you checked.]

[Explanation: the facts you found, stated without blame; the customer's part, in positive
terms: "The unit's log shows it ran above its rated load for six hours on March 3, which
the warranty doesn't cover."]

[What you will do: "We will repair it at our cost for parts, and waive the $150 service
fee."]

[How to proceed, with a date, and a goodwill close.]
```

If you apologize, make it sincere, take responsibility, name the specific error, and say
what will change. Words that admit fault (such as "defective") can carry legal weight, so
check with a manager or counsel when unsure.
