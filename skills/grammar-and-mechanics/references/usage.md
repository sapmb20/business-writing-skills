# Usage reference: punctuation, mechanics, confused words

Part of `grammar-and-mechanics` (revision pass 7). The SKILL.md body holds the rules that
catch most errors; this file holds the rest.

> **Source and license.** Written for this repo (CC BY 4.0, see LICENSE-CONTENT). The rules
> are standard American English usage, consistent with the APA's free style pages
> (https://apastyle.apa.org) and the Chicago Manual of Style's free Q&A
> (https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org). Where the two disagree, this file says so. A house
> style always wins.

## Punctuation, mark by mark

| Mark | Use it to | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Period** | End a statement or a mild command; follow some abbreviations (U.S., a.m.) | One period when a sentence ends in an abbreviation |
| **Question mark** | End a direct question | Not after an indirect question ("She asked whether the fund closed.") |
| **Exclamation mark** | Strong emphasis, rarely | Almost never in business writing; never two |
| **Comma** | Separate clauses, introductory elements, nonessential elements, series items, coordinate adjectives | See the never-rules in SKILL.md §4 |
| **Semicolon** | Join two related independent clauses; separate series items that contain commas | Not before a dependent clause or a list |
| **Colon** | Introduce a list, an explanation, or a quotation after a complete clause; formal salutations; ratios and times (3:1, 9:30) | Not after a verb or preposition ("The options are cash, stock, or both.") |
| **Em dash (—)** | Emphasis, an abrupt turn, or a parenthetical that contains commas | No spaces around it in APA and Chicago; at most one pair per sentence |
| **En dash (–)** | Ranges (2024–2026, pages 3–9); compound adjectives with an open compound (post–World War II) | Don't mix with "from" or "between" ("from 2024 to 2026") |
| **Hyphen (-)** | Compound modifiers before a noun; some prefixes; spelled-out numbers (twenty-one) | Not after an *-ly* adverb; check the dictionary for closed compounds |
| **Apostrophe** | Possession; contractions | Never for plurals (KPIs, 1990s); *its* has none |
| **Quotation marks** | Direct quotations; titles of short works; a word used in a special sense, once | US style puts commas and periods inside; never for emphasis |
| **Parentheses** | De-emphasized asides; abbreviations at first use; numbered items in a sentence: (a), (b) | A full sentence inside parentheses takes its own period only if it stands alone |
| **Brackets** | Your insertions inside a quotation ("[the company] reported..."); [sic] after an original error | Not as a second level of parentheses in prose, except in legal and APA nesting |
| **Ellipsis (...)** | Omitted words inside a quotation | Never change the meaning; keep a period before it if a sentence ends |
| **Slash (/)** | "Per" in units (km/h); alternatives in fixed pairs (and/or, sparingly) | Prefer words in prose ("cost per unit", "the chair or the vice chair") |

## Numbers and mechanics, beyond SKILL.md §5

- **Two numbers side by side:** spell out one ("twelve 5-year bonds").
- **Fractions:** spell out simple ones in prose ("two-thirds of the board"); numerals for
  mixed numbers (3 1/2) and in technical copy.
- **Decimals:** a zero before the point for values below one (0.75), except for
  quantities that can't exceed one (p < .05 in APA).
- **Ordinals:** first through ninth in words, 10th and above in numerals.
- **Measurements:** numerals with units (5 mm, 12 kg); a space between number and unit
  (except %, °, and currency symbols).
- **Addresses:** numerals for building numbers; spell out numbered streets up to nine
  (Fifth Avenue); state postal codes in addresses (MA), full names in prose.
- **Telephone numbers:** one consistent format, e.g. (555) 555-0100 or 555-555-0100;
  +1 for international readers.
- **Plurals of compounds:** pluralize the main word (attorneys general, sisters-in-law).
- **Italics:** titles of long works (books, journals, films); legal case names; words used
  as words ("the word *material* has a legal meaning"); sparingly for emphasis. Not for
  common Latin abbreviations (etc., e.g., i.e.).
- **Capitalization after a colon:** APA capitalizes a complete sentence after a colon;
  Chicago lowercases it unless two or more sentences follow. Pick one and be consistent.
- **Titles in headings:** title case (major words capitalized) or sentence case, one style
  per document.

## More confused words

| Pair | The difference |
| --- | --- |
| accept / except | receive; leave out |
| adverse / averse | harmful (adverse effects); unwilling (risk-averse) |
| capital / capitol | money, a city, an uppercase letter; the legislative building |
| complement / compliment | complete or go with; praise |
| continual / continuous | repeated with breaks; without a break |
| council / counsel | a group; advice or a lawyer |
| disinterested / uninterested | impartial; not interested |
| elicit / illicit | draw out; illegal |
| eminent / imminent | distinguished; about to happen |
| farther / further | physical distance; extent or degree (further analysis) |
| fiscal / financial | relating to government revenue or a fiscal year; relating to money generally |
| flaunt / flout | show off; break a rule openly |
| historic / historical | important in history; relating to the past (historical returns) |
| lay / lie | put something down (takes an object); recline |
| liable / likely | legally responsible; probable |
| precede / proceed | come before; go on |
| stationary / stationery | not moving; paper |
| than / then | comparison; time |
| who / whom | subject (who approved it); object (to whom it was sent) |
| who's / whose | who is; possessive |
| your / you're | possessive; you are |
| yield / return | income relative to price; total gain or loss, including price change |
