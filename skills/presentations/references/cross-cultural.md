# Cross-cultural audiences

Part of `presentations`, section 10.

- Organization, courtesy, and ideas about efficiency vary between cultures. Directness that
  sounds efficient in the U.S. can sound blunt elsewhere.
- **Avoid idioms, slang, jargon, humor, irony, sarcasm, and U.S.-specific allusions.** Don't
  use contractions or abbreviations that may be unclear. Speak in clear, complete sentences.
- Write dates out, give time zones, and use metric units where possible.
- Visuals carry culture too: colors (red is luck in some places, danger in others),
  gestures, symbols (a red cross can read as religious), how people are depicted, and
  reading direction. Use neutral colors and simple, universal icons (ISO symbols), and
  explain logos.
- When you can, test the talk and its visuals with someone from the audience's culture.
