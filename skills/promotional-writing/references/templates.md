# Promotional templates

Part of `promotional-writing`. Two fill-in templates: a press release and a sales letter.

> **Source and license.** Compiled for this repo (CC BY 4.0, see LICENSE-CONTENT). The
> wording is new; each element is credited in the "How this was compiled" table under its
> template. The sources are cited, not copied: one is licensed CC BY-NC and one is
> copyrighted, so neither is reproduced. The sales letter's structure also follows
> *Communication at Work* by Julie Rivers (eCampusOntario, 2022), section 8.4,
> https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/comm1062/, licensed
> [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Finance-specific rules are in
> `promotional-writing` section 8.

## 1. Press release

```
[Organization letterhead or logo]

PRESS RELEASE
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE                  (or "EMBARGOED UNTIL [date, time, time zone]")
[Month D, YYYY]

Media contact:
[Name], [title]
[Phone] | [email]

[Headline: subject-verb-object, specific, under about 12 words]
[Optional subhead: the key number or the second fact]

[CITY, State] — [Lead: who, what, when, where, and why, in one or two sentences.
Everything an editor needs if they print only this paragraph.]

[Second paragraph: the most important supporting detail: numbers, scope, availability.]

"[Quote that adds meaning or perspective, not facts already stated]," said [Full name],
[title] of [organization].

[Further detail, in decreasing order of importance, so an editor can cut from the bottom.
Optional second quote from a third party: a customer, partner, or participant.]

About [Organization]
[Boilerplate, one short paragraph: what the organization does, for whom, where, and its
size or founding year. Link to the website.]

[For a public company: forward-looking-statements note, if needed; see section 8.]

###
```

One page if possible. If it runs over, don't split a paragraph across pages; put "more" at
the bottom of the first page.

Before sending: every fact checked; names and titles spelled as their owners spell them;
the contact will answer the phone that day; addressed to a named editor or reporter.

**How this was compiled**

| Element | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Letterhead; "Press Release" label; "For immediate release" or "under embargo until"; date; contact block | Roberts-Crews, *Writing for Strategic Communication Industries* (Ohio State University, 2016), ch. 8, "Press release structure and format", https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/stratcommwriting/chapter/press-release-structure-and-format/ (CC BY-NC 4.0; cited, not copied) · Texas Commission on the Arts, "Press Release Template", https://www.arts.texas.gov/resources/tools-for-results/marketing/press-release-template/ |
| Headline, optional subhead, dateline, summary lead | Both sources above |
| Inverted pyramid | Roberts-Crews, ch. 8 |
| Quotes; the optional second quote from a third party | Roberts-Crews recommends at least two quotes, one of them from a third party; this template keeps the second as optional. |
| Boilerplate ("About us") at the end | Both sources |
| One page; "more" at the page break; "###" end mark | Both sources |
| Contact reachable after the release goes out | Texas Commission on the Arts |
| Headline length; forward-looking-statements note | Repo convention (`promotional-writing` section 8, from SEC rules) |

## 2. Sales letter or sales email

```
Subject: [The reader's benefit, specific: "Close your books in 3 days, not 6"]

Dear [Name]:

[Attention: open on the reader's problem or goal, not on your company:
 "Most finance teams of your size spend six working days on month-end close."]

[Interest: the product as the answer, with its central selling feature and one concrete
detail, written from the reader's side ("you").]

[Desire: two or three benefits (what the reader gets), each backed by proof: a figure, a
named client who agreed to be cited, a trial, a guarantee. Answer the likeliest objection.]

[Action: one easy step, the price, and a date: "Book a 20-minute demo at [link] before
June 30 and the first three months are free." State the price in the smallest sensible
unit, next to the benefit it buys.]

Sincerely,
[Name, title, direct phone]

P.S. [Restate the offer or the deadline in one line.]
```

Before sending: every claim can be substantiated; testimonials and endorsements follow the
FTC's rules; a commercial email includes a working opt-out and a valid postal address.

**How this was compiled**

| Element | Source |
| --- | --- |
| AIDA order: attention, interest, desire, action | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| Attention on the reader's problem or the solution's benefit | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| Central selling feature; the "you" view | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| Two or three supporting features; proof, testimonials, guarantees; answering objections | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| Easy action step; price stated in smaller units; reward for acting promptly | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| One unsupported claim undermines the whole letter | *Communication at Work* 8.4.3 |
| **Departure:** the source also describes fear appeals and psychological pricing (charm pricing, anchoring). This repo leaves them out, because `promotional-writing` holds finance marketing to the SEC and FINRA standard: fair, balanced, and not misleading. | Repo convention |
| Testimonials and endorsements | FTC, *Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising*, 16 CFR Part 255 |
| Opt-out and postal address in commercial email | CAN-SPAM Act, 15 U.S.C. § 7704; FTC, "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business" |
| The P.S. line | Repo convention |
