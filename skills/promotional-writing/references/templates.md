# Promotional templates

Part of `promotional-writing`. Two fill-in templates: a press release and a sales letter.

> **Source and license.** Written for this repo (CC BY 4.0, see LICENSE-CONTENT). The press
> release follows the long-standing newswire format (contact block, release line, dateline
> lead, inverted pyramid, boilerplate, end mark); the sales letter follows the AIDA model
> (attention, interest, desire, action), a public advertising model dating to the early
> twentieth century. Finance-specific rules are in `promotional-writing` section 8.

---

## 1. Press release

```
[Organization letterhead or logo]

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE                  (or "EMBARGOED UNTIL [date, time, time zone]")

Media contact:
[Name], [title]
[Phone] | [email]

[Headline: subject-verb-object, specific, under about 12 words]
[Optional subhead: the key number or the second fact]

[CITY, State], [Month D, YYYY] — [Lead: who, what, when, where, and why, in one or two
sentences. Everything an editor needs if they print only this paragraph.]

[Second paragraph: the most important supporting detail: numbers, scope, availability.]

"[Quote that adds meaning or perspective, not facts already stated]," said [Full name],
[title] of [organization].

[Further detail, in decreasing order of importance, so an editor can cut from the bottom.]

About [Organization]
[Boilerplate, 2-4 sentences: what the organization does, for whom, where, and its size or
founding year. Link to the website.]

[For a public company: forward-looking-statements note, if needed; see section 8.]

###
```

Before sending: every fact checked; names and titles spelled as their owners spell them;
the contact will answer the phone that day; addressed to a named editor or reporter.

## 2. Sales letter or sales email

```
Subject: [The reader's benefit, specific: "Close your books in 3 days, not 6"]

Dear [Name]:

[Attention: open on the reader's problem or goal, not on your company:
 "Most finance teams of your size spend six working days on month-end close."]

[Interest: the product as the answer, in one or two sentences, with one concrete detail.]

[Desire: two or three benefits (what the reader gets), each backed by proof: a figure, a
named client who agreed to be cited, a trial, a guarantee. Put the price next to the
benefit it buys, in the smallest sensible unit.]

[Action: one easy step with a date: "Book a 20-minute demo at [link] before June 30 and
the first three months are free."]

Sincerely,
[Name, title, direct phone]

P.S. [Restate the offer or the deadline in one line.]
```

Before sending: every claim can be substantiated; testimonials and endorsements follow the
FTC's rules; a commercial email includes a working opt-out and a postal address
(CAN-SPAM).
