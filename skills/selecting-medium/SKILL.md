# Selecting the Medium

Step 1.4 of the `business-writing` sequence. The medium is a decision, not a default: the
same message can succeed as a call and fail as an email. Choose it **after** purpose and
audience are settled (steps 1.1 and 1.2), because those two drive the choice more than
anything else.

Sources: standard business-communication practice (see ATTRIBUTION.md at the repo root).
The combined talk-then-write rule and the medium-mapping note are this skill's own.

## When to Use

- Before drafting, to confirm the message should be written at all
- When the user asks "should I email or call?", "should this be a meeting?", or "how do I
  tell them this?"
- When the content is sensitive, confidential, urgent, or going to many people
- When a written exchange has stalled or created a misunderstanding

For a full drafting task, start with `business-writing`; it calls this skill at step 1.4.

---

## 1. Weigh the six factors

Six factors decide most cases. Work through each one, and note which of them decide the case:

1. **Audience preferences and expectations.** How does this reader normally communicate,
   and what will they expect for a message of this kind?
2. **Organizational practices and policies.** Some organizations mandate a channel for
   announcements, approvals, or records.
3. **Breadth of distribution.** One person, a team, the whole organization, or the public?
4. **Urgency.** Does it need an answer in minutes, today, or whenever convenient?
5. **Sensitivity or confidentiality.** Who else could see it, forward it, or be legally
   entitled to it?
6. **The writer's own strengths.** Some people persuade better on paper, others in conversation.

The choice is partly personal, not only a matter of what each medium does.

## 2. Apply the two governing rules

- **To work something out, talk.** A call or a meeting settles in minutes what a chain of
  emails drags out over days.
- **For precise wording or a record, write.** When the exact words matter, or the message
  is complex or sensitive and needs a record, a written medium is usually essential.

When both apply, use both in sequence: resolve it in conversation, then send a short written
recap of what was decided.

**Sensitive information.** You rarely know how the other organization
stores its email or what its data policy says. Before emailing something very sensitive,
ask whether it should be in email at all. If not, schedule a call.

**Bad news.** Bad news for your boss is usually best delivered in person or by call, with
solutions ready, then recorded in writing. Bad news for customers or employees is often a
letter or email, because it needs a record and careful wording. Either way, structure it
with `delivering-bad-news`.

## 3. What each medium is for

| Medium | Best for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Email** | The default for work messages and files, inside and outside the organization | It can be forwarded in seconds and may be disclosed in litigation. Reread before sending |
| **Memo** | Internal announcements, policies, instructions, and short reports that need a record | A formal heading; often sent as an email attachment (`memo-writing`) |
| **Letter** | Formal messages to people outside the organization: offers, recommendations, legal or official notices | Letterhead and a real signature carry weight and formality |
| **Text or chat** (SMS, Slack, Teams) | Quick, simple coordination with people you already work with | Only if the reader is available now; never for anything that needs a record or nuance |
| **Phone or video call** | Discussion, nuance, tone, sensitive topics, clearing up confusion | Leaves no record, so follow up in writing when something is decided |
| **Voice mail** | One short point and a callback request | Move anything complex to email |
| **One-on-one meeting** | Building a relationship, hard conversations, complex or contested issues | The most expensive in time |
| **Group meeting** | Brainstorming, joint problem-solving, decisions that need buy-in | Needs an agenda and someone taking minutes |
| **Web conference or webinar** | Groups in several locations, training, large briefings | Plan the technology and the interaction (`virtual-presentations`) |
| **Intranet page** | Policies, procedures, directories, and news for employees | Internal only, but assume it can leak |
| **Public website** | Product information, news releases, FAQs, careers, investor relations | Everything is public and permanent |
| **Social media** | Professional networking and promotion | Public, permanent, and often regulated in finance (`blogs-and-social-media`) |

To write for these last three, load `writing-for-the-web` (intranet and website pages, FAQs)
or `blogs-and-social-media` (posts, forums); for press releases and other promotion, load
`promotional-writing`.

Media overlap and keep changing with technology. Map a newer tool to the function it
performs: Slack and Teams chat are instant messaging; Zoom is a web conference; a shared
wiki or SharePoint page is an intranet.

## 4. Easy to confuse

- **Memo vs. letter:** a memo stays inside the organization; a letter goes outside it, on
  letterhead, with a signature.
- **Text vs. instant message:** texting is phone to phone, for people on the move; IM is a
  real-time exchange that needs the other person available now.
- **Phone call vs. voice mail:** a call is for interaction and tone; voice mail carries one
  brief point.
- **Web conference vs. videoconference:** a web conference runs on everyday apps; a formal
  videoconference needs high-end equipment and often professional services.
- **Individual vs. group meeting:** one-on-one builds a relationship or handles a
  controversial issue; a group meeting brainstorms and decides.
- **Intranet vs. public website:** internal sharing vs. customers and clients.

---

## 5. Delivering the recommendation

- Name the medium in the first line, with the one or two factors that decided it.
- If a second medium should follow (a call, then a written recap), say so and say why.
- If the answer is a written medium, hand off: email goes to `email-writing`, and
  everything else returns to step 2 of `business-writing`.
- If the right medium is a conversation, offer a short agenda or talking points instead of
  a draft. Don't draft the email the user shouldn't send.
